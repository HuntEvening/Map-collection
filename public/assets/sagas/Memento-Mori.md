Slowly, Felen opened his eyes, expecting the normal sounds of the camp waking up, but the weather was unforgiving. The wind gripped his tent, shook it so hard that a few of the pegs holding it up in one corner had come loose, making one side of the tent start to fall in on itself. He turned to look at the flap that served as a makeshift door. It, too, had come loose and was shaking violently. Looking out through the flap's small window, now flying open, he expected to see the camp in its usual sombre morning state, but it was now buzzing. People were running from tent to tent, grabbing piles of paper. Some were searching for replacement armour, others simply trying to put theirs on in a panic. The sharp stench of sweat, wet leather, and nervous piss, clung to the air, thickening the already stifling atmosphere. Felen slowly dragged himself out of bed, his body aching from the hard cot. He grabbed his coat from a messy pile of personal possessions and walked out through the tent flap. If the weather had seemed rough inside, it was nothing compared to outside. As soon as he left the somewhat safe tent, the storm grabbed at him as well, pulling at his messy bun and his coat. He was met by a wall of cold rain, feeling like small needles poking all over his face. The rain wasn't heavy enough to warrant a second, thicker coat, but still enough to soak him if he didn't fold up the collar of the coat and find his way into the safety of another tent quickly. Grey skies turned the camp into a palette of muddied steel and ash. Only the occasional lantern flame defied the gloom, flickering defiantly in the wind. Looking around the camp with squinted eyes, it was difficult to find where the centre of all commotion was, but he managed to spot one of his superiors through the crowd. His brows were furrowed, and though just as stressed as the others, there was determination in his stride compared to their frantic running. It was clear he was aiming for one specific tent, pushing through the masses. One unfortunate private got in his way and, in the middle of getting a scolding and a finger waved in his face, the superior locked eyes with Felen. He just stood there, finger still raised towards the now scared private. The superior snapped out of it, cupping a hand around his mouth and shouting what Felen could only assume was
"Captain Corwood! Get over here!"

Entering the tent was like stepping into a beehive. Everyone was shouting over one another, reaching for paperwork, maps, and figurines, all of it gathered on the long table in the middle of the tent. Felen pushed himself just a little closer to the table to see what it was all about. There, in the middle, they had laid out the worn map. He had seen this map many times before, with previous attempts drawn across it, and even wine stains from the times the general had slammed the table so hard in a fit of rage that the cups toppled over. As he remembered this, he looked over to the end of the table, expecting to see the general standing there, barking orders, but the man sitting there was only a shell of whom he had once been. He was the centre of all the commotion, yet the only calm presence in the tent. He sat slumped in his chair, head leaning on his fist, eyes staring far off into the distance, as if his mind were no longer present. "We're being led by a ghost," Felen thought grimly. Before anyone could speak, the tent flap burst open, nearly torn off its stitching. A courier staggered in, soaked through, one arm clutched tight around a scroll sealed in gold wax. He didn't speak. He simply set the scroll on the table, in front of the general, eyes darting around as if afraid to be blamed for its contents. The general didn't even acknowledge the letter in front of him, his gaze still in the far distance. Slowly the room fell silent all staring at the letter expecting the general to do something. Felen stepped forward as no one else moved. He broke the seal and read aloud:
*"To the scattered remnants of the so-called Alliance,"*  
*"You are surrounded. The war is over. Your Alliance have surrendered."*
*"Other armies have laid down their weapons already"*
*"If you do the same you may be spared"*
*"Resist, and none will walk away from what comes next."*
The silence that followed was thick enough to choke on. The general didn't even look at the letter. He shifted his weight, eyes still glazed, and muttered:
"Cowards. You think I'd beg those knife-eared bastards for mercy?" He rose with a slow, deliberate creak of his chair, and pointed at the map like it meant something. "We hold the south line. We make them bleed for every inch they take." Murmurs rippled through the officers, not with agreement, only fear. Felen felt the cold weight in his stomach return. This wasn't courage. This was suicide. Still he said nothing. He watched as the room twisted itself into false resolve, as if pretending hard enough would make the message untrue. As if sheer stubbornness could beat precision and magic and fire. He thought again of his daughter. Her fingers stained with crushed berries. Her laugh, lighter than birdsong. If this was their final stand, he would not waste it on a fool's pride. Before the room could collapse into hollow agreement, a voice cut through the silence.
"Sir... with respect," came the brittle voice of Lieutenant Harven, a younger officer, barely into his twenties. He stood straighter than he likely felt, hands clenched at his sides. "We won't survive this. We don't have the numbers, or the mages. The men out there are starving. Half don't have boots, let alone weapons. If we fight... we'll die." The general turned, slowly, like someone waking from a long dream. His eyes were no longer distant. They were cold. 
"You'll die either way, Lieutenant." He stepped around the table with the slow, grinding weight of a man who had long since stopped caring about hope.
"If you die on the field, they'll write your name on a stone somewhere. Say you died with honour. But if you turn your back on this army, on *me*, I will personally drag your cowardly arse into the mud and put a blade through your neck. You. Every single deserter. I'll make the gods weep for the waste of your lives." Harven didn't speak again. No one did. Felen stared at the map. The lines. The arrows. The figurines. It had never felt more like a child's game. 

No one argued again. The general gave a curt nod. Nothing more. The war room emptied in silence. Felen stepped out last. The camp had changed. What had once been chaotic was now... still. But not peaceful. It was the stillness of knowing. Of being told. Officers peeled off in different directions, returning to their squads. At first, the whispers were scattered and confused, small clusters forming, arguments flaring, words like *“surrender,” “trap,” “mercy”* crackling through the air like sparks. A captain barked back at a protesting tiefling, silencing him with a single look. Another officer was shouting, until the squad he addressed simply turned their backs and began putting on their armour. It didn't take long. The shouting stopped. The muttering faded. And what followed was worse. Quiet. Not silence, exactly. The low sounds of war preparation filled the air: the creak of leather straps, blades being drawn and checked, shields tested with a few slow, nervous taps. Someone adjusted a helm. Someone else knelt to tighten a bootlace. The transition unsettled Felen more than the shouting had. People began moving with slow, mechanical rhythm, not as though they had accepted death, but as if they had *absorbed* it. Like a fever that had broken. He passed one soldier who still looked confused, armour half-on, eyes flicking between his hands like he didn't remember what to do with them. Another sat with his back against a crate, staring at nothing, mouth moving in a prayer that never ended. Someone nearby vomited in the mud, quietly. No one reacted. Felen walked through it all. A priestess wandered past, muttering a psalm of Asyuo under her breath. Her tabard was stiff with dried blood, the words lost somewhere between ritual and panic. She didn't look up. He stopped near his own tent. His coat lay folded atop his cot, soaked from earlier rain. He didn't reach for it. He just stood there. Somewhere beyond the hills, a horn sounded,low and clear. The elves, signalling readiness. Felen clenched his jaw until it ached. His eyes burned, but no tears came. This was the end. Everyone knew it. And yet, still they marched. Still they prepared. Still they had to hold the line, because they were told to. 

The march to the field was wordless. Felen walked among them, shoulder to shoulder with soldiers he barely knew, all clad in mismatched armour, soaked through and clinking with every step. No banners flew. No war cries were shouted. Only the sound of boots in wet mud and the low thud of heartbeats no one could hear but their own. The path to the battlefield wound past graves they hadn't had time to dig, past old barricades half-collapsed in the muck. Felen's eyes drifted to each broken shield, each shattered helmet, wondering which of them would mark his own end. They crested the final ridge. And there it was. There had been many clashes on this battlefield, the ground was torn up, bones and weapons scattered and slowly decaying in deep puddles left by the rain. There was no chance, after an encounter, to return and recover the bodies. They had tried at first, but there were too many dormant magical traps lying in wait, and no one could be sure what spell components might have been left in the robes of the fallen mages. After a few attempts, more soldiers were killed than recovered, stepping unknowingly into arcane snares. Now, the bodies were left for the desperate few mages who dared to raise them through necromancy, knowing full well they would face trial after the war.

Felen felt the same desperation. Many of his fellow clerics had spent countless hours praying to Asyuo, hoping to gain some favour in the war. For himself, he preferred to pray to Lyx, and to Lirrus on occasion. Lyx was, after all, the one who judged them all for what they had done in this damn war. On darker days like this one, he preferred to pray to Lirrus, just for the hope of seeing another sunrise. But lately, there were no answers, not even a hint of their presence. The other clerics always seemed relieved when they received theirs. At times, he would simply sit and observe them as they prayed, and watched how their faces shifted, from grim and stained to calm and unburdened. It was if they had been forgiven, allowed to forget the harm they'd caused in that day's battle. But now, standing here on the battlefield, with the rain insisting on soaking him through his armour, he felt compassion for the mages who had turned to necromancy. Because, like them, he too only wanted to get out of this alive, and he wanted nothing more than that for himself. The dream of returning home to his wife and child, it was all that kept him upright.

He muttered a prayer to Lirrus, asking for just one more day to live, just to see the sun rise again. He pictured his wife's face lit by those wonderful sun rays on a summer's morning, her skin glowing like amber and honey. He recalled his daughter running through a field, picking flowers for her mother to take home. But, just like all the other times he had tried, there was nothing. Just the sound of nervous movement, of rain hitting the weapons and armour around him, and the faint sound of someone crying in the distance. All he could think of was this moment, standing here now, where his own superiors, in their hubris, were going to force them all to die for nothing. The war was lost, and the least the gods could do was answer his final prayer. But no, not even that would they grant him. He looked up at the horizon, and along his entire field of view was one long line of elves. He could faintly see their glowing weapons through the rain. He felt his blood drain from his limbs, his heart sinking to his stomach. This must be what those necromancer mages felt. The primal need for survival. The search for any way out. Like a trapped animal gnawing at its own leg, only making the pain worse.

Behind him, someone barked out the order to advance, and the line behind him began to push. All of those who had resisted this last call to arms were placed in the middle, so they’d be forced into the battle regardless. And now, in the chaos of panicked soldiers surging forward, he was shoved to the side while the rest marched on. He began to lead his own company forward, slowly, through the treacherous mud and deep puddles, telling them,
“If you’ve any last prayers to make, better do it now.” At the front, the army was already being hit by spells. If not for the screams of pain, one could almost find the myriad of colours crossing the battlefield... beautiful. With each step closer to the enemy, he kept repeating in his head, 
“I want to live. I want to live.” He thought about his family, his friends. All the ones they had already lost. The war was supposed to be over. Other armies on their side had already surrendered, and yet here they were, walking toward their own slaughter, stepping over bones protruding from the mud. Bones that used to belong to their comrades. Men and women they had cared for.

He was too focused on not falling to keep his eyes on the enemy line. If it hadn’t been for the fizzing sound of the fireball tearing through the rain, he wouldn’t have had a chance to throw himself into the mud to dodge it. He stayed down, letting the ringing in his ears fade, listening instead for nearby cries for help. Screams to his left. Screams behind and to the right. The left was closer. Keeping an eye out so he wouldn’t get trampled, he crawled toward the sound. The closer he got, the stronger the stench of burnt flesh became. He held out his right hand over the soldier's charred leg and chanted a spell, just enough to close the worst of the wound, then bandaged over it to save his energy for others. He looked up, scanning the field. So many already dead, hundreds if not even thousands. It was hard to tell which are the newly dead from the ones killed in previous clashes, a pure and deliberate massacre. The soldier who had screamed behind him was gone. Once again, his mind betrayed him, he saw his wife, her face warm in morning light. He tried to get up, but slipped again in the mud. The fight had only just begun, and he was already spent. No matter what he did, no matter how many he saved, they would all die anyway. He just wanted to live. Just this one battle. One more time. Just one more prayer... 

He opened his mouth to pray to Lyx, but the words caught in his throat. He couldn’t remember them. For the first time, the prayer was simply... gone. But as he prepared to mouth the old prayers to Lirrus, he stopped. None of the gods had answered before... why would they now? He slammed his first into the mud out of frustration before he murmured in a low voice,
"If you won’t listen, then I’ll give myself to whoever will." Pausing and he took one long breath, 
“To any god listening, I’ll do anything to live through this battle. Take anything, just let me go home. You can even take my soul if you want it... Just let me survive this.” At first, it was like all the other times, no answer. Nothing but rain and steel and weeping. Until the sound began to fade away. Around him, the mud froze slightly, as it would on a frost-bitten morning. A cold feeling crept down his back, raising every hair along his skin. A hand settled on Felen's shoulder. The grasp was colder than frostbite yet free of pain. Instead, it carried a sense of care and understanding. Felen looked up to see a pale man with glowing green eyes, bright as polished emeralds, smiling down at him. The smile assuring him he'll be alright. 
*“Deal.”* The pale man speaks in a low, smooth, almost playful, tone of voice; before snapping his finger and turning into nothing but a black mist. As he faded, so did the cold. But in its place came the pain, a head splitting headache, he tried to get up on one knee so he could get away, but it was as if all his strength had left his body with the cold. His vision blurred, darkness pressing at the edges. He barely had time to scream before the world vanished in a burst of white noise and lightning behind his eyes.

---

Waking, Felen's whole body ached. He wondered how the sleeping cots could keep getting worse over time. He pulled the covers closer around him. His entire body felt like it was freezing to the bone. But instead of slipping back into blissful sleep, a frantic voice started calling for him.
"Captain? You awake? Please say that you’re fine." The voice was low and tired. At first it sounded like it was miles away, but it slowly came into focus. Felen turned and opened his eyes to the worried face of his fellow medic, Lerran.
"Did I dream it all? Did we really fight the elves again?" Felen asked, his throat hoarse, craving something to drink.
"Unfortunately. And you, out of all people, should be dead, Captain."
"Why? What happened? Did we lose?" Felen shot up in the bed but fell back down, his entire body weaker than he thought and a low, lingering headache worsening with the effort.
"Don’t you remember anything from the battle, Captain? We won!" Lerran's face lit up. "We won because of you! Well... we surrendered, eventually, after the general went down. But..."
"Slow down!" Felen burst out. "I have no memory at all of what happened. From..." Flashes returned, the dead on the field, the screams, the sound of spells flying past him.
"Last I remember, I healed someone. Then it all went blank."
"Okay, so, I didn’t see it all, but Veyra did. She saw you lying on the ground, then suddenly, you shot up, charging the elves that were advancing, and you stood there alone..." Lerran waved his hands around, struggling to explain. "Marrek, you know him from Cael's company, said lightning was flying off you. You dragged elves straight to you, then just... tore through them. No one really saw how. Some say it looked like Rag’s own fury took over. Thess ran in after you, tried to guard your back. She swears you melted through someone’s armour like it was paper. But… well, she got hit in the head. Claims you healed her with no effort, but how it was cold feeling, not your usual calming warm one, but you know, it’s the head, we’re not sure if she’s all there. Oh, and the general? Got himself killed. Magic missile, stray shot. Just like that." Lerran shrugged.
"After you tore through maybe two dozen of them, the elves surrendered. We sent a messenger. Told them we just want to leave. Peacefully." Felen blinked. Once. Twice. No words came.
"You really don’t remember anything, do you?" Lerran's voice was both confused and worried. Felen tried, tried hard, to pull something back. Nothing. Just one vision: blood, all over him, all around him. Piles of dead elves, and a feeling of joy seeing the slaughter. 
"I don’t remember," he said. Lerran stared at him.  
"Yeah. That’s what scares me."
