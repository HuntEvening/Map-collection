A minty freshness lingered on her skin where the healer's hands had been. With pleading eyes, Elithana looked up at the young human cleric, met with a look that tried to be comforting. She had already seen the same type of face at the previous healers she had visited while in Timmeria. The healer spoke up with a gentle voice: 
"Your body is healthy. I've tried every healing spell and curse detection I know. Are you absolutely sure there's nothing else you can tell me?" Elithana tried to recall whether she had already told the healer or not, but all of her visits to different healers and herbalists were becoming a blur, the same spells and questions asked over and over by a new face each time. 
"I honestly can't remember," she said with a tired sigh. The healer pulled out a chair and sat down.
"It's fine. Take some time to think about any abnormal signs you might have noticed on your travels here."

Recalling the past weeks after her departure from Mosshall had been nothing but painful. She tried asking every single person in the village for help, but she was ignored and sometimes even threatened. None dared to enter the crypt, so she had to make the difficult decision to travel onwards to find help, resulting in her having to travel along the river towards the mighty lakes. However, during her travels, she had to take increasingly longer breaks. She couldn't remember a single night when she could sleep in peace. The noises and pain had become increasingly worse; She shivered at the thought of the night when she could only writhe in pain because of the buzzing. She looked up at the healer again and answered:
"I think that was everything, if I've told you about my long journey here?" 
"Yes, you did," The healer then replied, "I'm sorry again that I couldn't help resurrect your friends. Perhaps another temple in the city can offer assistance. If not, may your friends' souls find peace." Elithana's thoughts linger for a bit before whispering
"Thank you," before slowly stepping out into the busy main street.

The moment she stepped out onto the street, she was met by a dense, damp fog hanging low over the harbour, swallowing the sounds of the bustling crowd. Waves lapped quietly against the docks, their gentle rhythm lost beneath the roar of merchants and the salty breeze. But even with all the busy talk from the people around her, her thoughts were still rushing, planning out where the last temple would be. In her mind, she went over the events in the crypt again and again, as if there were some key thing she had missed in it all. She passed by a busy tavern and looked up, studying a group of young sailors, probably enjoying their short time in port before daring the sea again. Watching them smile, she wondered when she had last smiled. She tried to remember what it felt like, but it brought back memories of how she and the others used to spend their time in taverns, telling stories and seeing who could drink more than Veacaan. *"Why me? Rogdan was always the one who saw through traps. Thalis could've got us out. He always did. He was the one who knew about things like this; he could have found a way to help them. Why couldn't it have been me instead?" Her thoughts began to spiral.  "It should have been me. Out of all the people who deserved it, I do."* A deep voice cut through her thoughts, 
"Do you need anything?" She blinked and realised she had been staring at the group of sailors, now silent and watching her. She didn't answer, turned away, and kept moving down the road. The sailors' murmurs faded into the clamour of the street, just another layer of noise with the merchants calling, gulls crying, boots slapping against wet stone. 
*"Wait… don't leave me."* She glanced back, but the group had already returned to their laughter, tankards raised in cheer. Maybe one of them had said it. Or maybe she was just tired. She shook her head. Just the static, only noise. As she started walking, she heard another voice, soft, low, almost in her ear: 
"Please… not the water." She stopped in her tracks; no one was near her. The buzzing in her ears grew again to an almost painful level. She forced herself to keep walking, repeating in her mind, *"Focus, need to get to the temple, focus."*

Along the way, she asked various people if they knew any healers, but the best she had got was a necromancer who shook his head, with a bitter laugh escaping him. 
"You're asking the impossible, calling back souls likely already recycled into the Weave." She had no more options but to seek the last temple. Through the thick fog, the temple appeared first as a vague glow, a blurred halo of gold. Only when she got closer did its shape emerge: pale stone walls softened by the harshness of the harbour's wind and rain. She must have been lucky since this was a temple for Lathander. If any good healers were willing to help, it would be here. She walked up towards the entrance with a little more hope in her chest, but a glimmer in the corner of her eye caught her attention. At the base of the steps was a shallow stone basin with a few coins that glinted faintly beneath the shallow water. But there were not only coins in the water; there was also a small coil of knotted rope and wilted flowers. Someone had left half a loaf of bread wrapped in linen, now soaked through and more dough than bread, and a small cloth bundle weighed down by a smooth river stone. She took out a copper coin of her own and added it to the bowl before she pushed the heavy door open.

The doors creaked as Elithana stepped inside. The temple of Lathander glowed in soft gold, light spilling through stained-glass lanterns in places all around the room. The smell of incense did its best to cover the constant smell of saltwater that clung to the air, almost chokingly sweet.
"Hello? Is anyone awake?" She asked, her eyes tracing the patterns of light dancing across the room. A small door at the end of the room opened, and a young acolyte appeared, rubbing sleep from their eyes. Their robe was simple, dyed a light yellow, almost the same shade as ancient parchment.
"Evening, traveller…" they said softly, dipping their head. "You should not be here at this hour. The temple is closed. If you return at dawn…"
"No," Elithana cut in, her voice sharper than she intended. She waited for the echoing word to die out before she continued, "I need to see the high cleric. Now! It's an emergency." The acolyte flinched, but didn't back away. They studied her. She had not had a single break since the crypt, so she wondered what she must look like to a stranger. She was unwashed, messy, and unkempt. The acolyte looked unsure. Then, the static buzzing rose again to a deafening level. She could see the acolyte's mouth move, but it did not match what she heard through the static. 
"*Who lit the candles?*" The voice was faint, almost childlike. "*I'm cold…*" The static died out as fast as it had come, leaving Elithana frozen in place, unsure if she'd imagined it. She looked around the room, searching for whoever could have mentioned the candles, but they were all alone. The acolyte saw her freeze. 
"Something wrong?" he asked with a worried tone. 
"I'm sorry. Didn't you hear that?" She glanced around, "Is there anyone else here?" The cleric, now confused at her change of topic, answered,  
"No, it's just us here," they said. "As I said, it's closed at this hour, and it's very late. The Dawnfather's chosen must rest before the morning light."
Elithana swallowed hard. Whatever it was, exhaustion or something worse, she couldn't afford to think about it now.
"My friends are dead," Elithana said. The words were as if something thick and bitter sat in her throat, refusing to move, as if even grief didn't want to leave her. "I've already spent many days travelling to get here, so if I wait until dawn, it might be too late." The acolyte hesitated, glancing towards the side hall where the clergy quarters must be. Then they sighed and gave a small, weary smile. 
"Very well. Wait here. I shall wake him."

The acolyte wandered off towards the door they had been eyeing before, leaving her alone. She stood there, listening to the soft hiss of burning candles and the distant crashing of waves outside. She wandered around the room, pretending to study the art and relics, when in reality she was trying to find where that child's voice might have come from. When she looked between the benches, she heard the sound of footsteps and arguing, mumbling voices coming closer to the door, and another, clearer but faint voice further down the rows of benches. 
*"Did the war end? Did we win...?"* This time, the voice sounded like an elderly woman. She rushed down the rows of benches to find where the voice was emanating from, but when she got to the point, there was nothing. That was when the door slammed open, and out stormed a cleric, with the acolyte, hovering close behind by the doorway, clearly nervous. He glanced between Elithana and the cleric, unsure if he should leave or stay. Even though it was late, and he was not formally dressed, the cleric's golden robes seemed to shimmer in the dim temple light. She walked through the rows of benches and up to him. He looked her over with a faint smile that didn't reach his eyes. 
"A drow," he murmured, as though confirming some private thought. "You've travelled far, I see. How… unfortunate that your companions could not keep up with you."
When Elithana started to speak, he raised a hand. "I understand your grief. But you must understand, resurrection is no simple healing. It is reserved for those who walk in the light. Tell me, child," his tone sharpened, "did they?" Elithana frowned, thrown off. 
"Did they what?"
"Walk in the light," he said, as though it were obvious. "Were they righteous? Devout? Worthy of the Dawnfather's grace? She looked back at the cleric and blinked at him, heart pounding. "They were, they were good people. They were better people than I've ever been. They didn't deserve this." Her voice cracked, and she hated how weak it sounded. "What does that even mean? What makes someone worthy? Who decides that?"
"The gods decide," he said coldly. "And the gods did not see fit to save them." Elithana's nails dug into her palms. She moved her hand over to the hilt of her rapier to avoid hurting her palms even more. The static buzzed faintly in her ears, rising and falling like distant waves. 
"You're saying it's too late? That there's nothing you can do?" she asked, voice on the verge of failing her.
"Too long, from what I can tell looking at you", he replied, voice sharp. "After ten days, resurrection magic needs more than just remains. At that point, you require a very strong magic wielder, and those are rare and spread far between." Her stomach dropped. 
"But they only just died," she whispered. "It's only been two weeks. I didn't know. I had to walk back... I couldn't carry them..."
"What you require, child," he laid a hand on her shoulder, "Is a miracle, or a god's personal interest. Neither comes cheaply." He folded his arms. "And such, you have my condolences. But you cannot ask the Dawnfather to mend what time and fate have already claimed." Her hands shook, and she gripped the hilt of her rapier even tighter, the only solid thing anchoring her. "No. There has to be something. What if you use me instead? Trade me. Do something. There has to be a way." The acolyte took a half step forward, with wide-eyed filled with pity, as if he wanted to intervene but stopped as the cleric raised his hand and shook his head, unflinching. 
"It doesn't work like that." Her breathing quickened, every word like a wound tearing open. "Everything that should've happened to me… happened to them instead." The cleric's gaze softened for a moment, but only in pity. "Then perhaps the Dawnfather spared you for a reason. Do not waste the life you still have."

Elithana stared at him, her pulse hammering in her ears. The static swelled to a roar, drowning out his words. Her fingers tightened on the hilt, and before she fully realised, she drew her rapier; the steel whispered as it cleared the scabbard, its tip quivering as she levelled it at his throat. The acolyte gasped and grabbed his holy symbol and muttered a prayer. The cleric didn't flinch. He merely regarded the blade at his throat with the same detached scrutiny as before, his hands clasped neatly before him. 
"Will you strike me down in the house of the Dawnfather?" he asked, his tone almost curious. "Will that bring them back, child?"  
"Don't," the acolyte stammered. "Please, lower your weapon. This isn't the way." Elithana's grip on the rapier tightened.  
"You call yourselves servants of the light," she spat, the words breaking as they left her throat. "And yet you won't even try."  
"There is no trying," the cleric said flatly. "The window has closed. Even if I wished to intervene, I do not have the power to wrest souls from where they've gone. And had the Dawnfather deemed them worthy, they would not have perished." The words echoed in her head, hollow and final. *Unworthy.* Something inside her broke. With a scream that tore from her throat before she even realised it, Elithana drove the rapier forward. There was a soft, wet resistance, then the sickening give of flesh as the blade sank into his throat. The cleric gasped, a sharp intake of breath, more surprise than pain. His eyes, wide for the first time, locked on hers. He tried to speak, but all that came out was a sickening bubbling sound and blood running down the edge of his lips. The acolyte shrieked, dropping their holy symbol as they stumbled backwards. 
"No, no, what have you done?!" Elithana's breath came in ragged bursts. She didn't even feel the weapon in her hands, didn't hear the static any more, only the roaring of blood in her ears. She turned her attention back, staring at the dying man. His lips moved faintly, as if in prayer. The temple was silent, save for the hiss of candles and the distant crash of waves.

Elithana's breath rasped in the stillness, the rapier trembling in her hands. She should have felt something like relief, horror, anything, but there was only the cold hatred. She had exhausted all of her options; there was no way to get them back, and this cleric dares to think that she should be happy about living. The cleric's lips moved one last time. Then the static swelled, not as a buzz now, but as words, clear and sharp, spilling into her mind.
*"Mercy… forgive me…"* Her eyes widened. She hadn't imagined it. Those were his last words, still clinging to him even as the life drained from his body. The acolyte whimpered by the door, breaking her trance. 
"Please… please don't kill me," he begged, voice cracking. His eyes darted to the cleric's corpse, then back to her blade. "I won't tell anyone. I swear it. Please, I"

A shudder crawled up her spine. She had heard this before. Outside the tavern. In the streets. In this temple. She had not imagined it. She blinked hard and took a long deep breath before answering. 
"If I should follow your master's advice and live on, then I need no witnesses." She hesitated before continuing, "I'm sorry," she whispered, though she wasn't sure if she meant it for him, or herself, or the gods watching.  Before he could scream, she crossed the room in two strides. The blade found his chest, quick and deep. He gasped, then slumped, his blood mixing with the cleric's across the temple floor. I prayed until the end"* Elithana staggered back, hands flying to her head as the whispers layered over each other. No longer static, but voices, dozens of them, sharp and pleading and real. She stumbled against a bench, the rapier slipping from her hand and clattering to the floor. They were all here. The cleric. The acolyte. The strangers in the streets. The child's voice asking about candles again, the old woman going on and on about waiting for her husband to come home from the war. She bit down hard on her lip to keep from screaming. She hadn't been haunted by a buzzing noise; it had been the layered voices of the dead.

Elithana didn’t remember sheathing the rapier. She didn’t remember stepping over the bodies or sneaking out through the temple doors. That night, Elithana found herself in a small cabin, its low ceiling pressing down on her like a coffin. She packed away the journal into its pocket in the backpack and brought out a small sewing kit. She sewed the pocket shut; he will always be with her, a reminder pressing against her back. Thalis was gone. Rogdan, Veacaan, all of them, gone in a way these spirits were not. They had moved on. Whatever had happened in that crypt, whatever had left her alive, had tethered her to this… this noise. Not her dead. Just the dead.
