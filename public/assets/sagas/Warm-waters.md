She prodded at the mutton stew with her spoon, searching for a tender chunk of meat among the potatoes and carrots. The fire crackled in the hearth, but the table was eerily quiet. Normally her mother, would fill the silence with sharp talk of farm matters, what sheep should be culled or sold, whether they needed shearing or moving to another field. Tonight there were only harsh glances, her crimson eyes fixed on her husband, who kept his gaze buried in his bowl. Her father hunched low over his food. Shadows deepened the tired lines on his face, his tail coiled tight around the bench leg, as if anchoring him against the storm brewing beside him.

Aleria frowned and shot her brother a questioning look, tilting her head toward their parents. He shrugged, and together they turned their attention back to their mother. At last, her father looked up, reluctantly meeting his wife's blazing red eyes. He cleared his throat.  
"Aleria…" He hesitated, fumbling for words. "Your mother and I have made arrangements for you to live at Eluna's temple." She could barely believe what she was hearing. She nearly sprang from her chair.  
"Is it true? I'll finally study at a temple? Will they teach me about all the gods? Will I learn their history? I want to hear their stories about the Roar, oh, and about Yol!"

Her voice gradually rose with excitement, but the moment she spoke the names, her mother’s hand slammed the table. The wooden bowls rattled, cups toppled, and broth splashed across the cloth. Her crimson eyes blazed, obsidian skin catching the firelight in a hard gleam.  
"QUIET! You do not speak of the devils at this table," she screamed, voice like a whip. "We are sending you there to stop this foolishness." Aleria sank into her chair, wishing she could sink straight through the floor. 
"Your questions about Rag and Yol have made the whole village think us cursed. We are done bearing the shame of you. The priests can have you now, let them make something of you, or break you if they must." her mother hissed. "This ends now. It is time you learned to understand that."

Aleria turned to her father, desperate for some hint of comfort,  but he only hunched lower, his horns catching the firelight as he kept his eyes on his bowl. He would not look at her. Not even once. She felt how the tears rose to her eyes and sadness stuck in her throat, but she bit together. No words would change her mother's mind, and her father's silence cut deeper than any scolding. They weren’t sending her to learn. They were just sending her away like a lamb too stubborn to herd. The room pressed in on her, the fire's heat suffocating, the smell of mutton suddenly sickening. She shoved her chair back, the scrape of wood on stone sharp in the silence, and bolted before anyone could stop her. The heavy farmhouse door slammed against the wall as she burst outside, the sound scattering a pair of gulls from the roof. Through the open door, she heard the fading screams from her mother, demanding her return.

The evening air rushed over her, cool and sharp with the smell of the briny ocean. The sky was ablaze with sunset, streaks of crimson and gold spilling over the cliffs to the ocean's restless surface. That light illuminated the fields, making it look like an ocean of grass and gold swaying in the wind. Sheep huddled together on the slopes, their bleating faint against the roar of the ocean below. She ran across the worn track, her boots thudding on turf and stone. At the far edge of the pasture, where the land broke away into jagged cliffs, she slipped down the narrow path she and her brother had claimed as their own. It twisted between small openings in bushes and rock until it opened onto their secret hollow. A crack in the cliffs opened to the sea, forming a narrow cove where the waves lapped gently instead of battering the stone. Pools gathered in the sheltered rocks, which filled each tide.

Aleria slipped off her boots and sat on the flat ledge above the shallows, lowering her feet into the water. It was warm, even though the surrounding air already carried the bite of summers end. The warmth spread through her legs and chest, smoothing out the sharp edges of her panic. She let her toes stir circles in the pool, over and over, until the world blurred to nothing but ripples. It was easier this way, to sink into the water, into the warmth, and let the shouting at the table feel far away. She learned forward, staring at the ripples around her ankles when she moved her feet around in circles. *"Yol is here,"* she thought. The old stories said Rag's fire lieutenant had fallen into these very waters, driven down by the tieflings of Erellia. The villagers called it cursed, but to her, it was proof. Proof that the myths were real. She trailed her fingers across the surface of the rocks, shivering at the thought that she was touching a remnant of Yol's power. Maybe he still burned down there, chained beneath the waves, keeping her feet warm in defiance of the world. For a while she let herself believe she wasn't cursed or evil, only chosen to notice what others feared. Out here, away from her mother's scorn and her father's silence, she could dream that the gods and devils whispered to her alone.

A gull flew overhead, its cry cutting through the hush of the cove. Aleria's toes traced circles in the warm pool, her thoughts still wrapped in fire and myth, when the crunch of boots on stone made her look up. Ervius stood at the lip of the hollow, arms folded, the sinking sun painting the edges of his horns to molten copper. The same light caught his ashy white hair, much like her own, and turned it into a pale halo. He had shot up in height over the past year, taller now than most grown men, yet he carried it awkwardly, shoulders held too stiff, his stance a little too deliberate. He looked as though he wanted to appear imposing, but there was still too much boy left in him for the role to sit comfortably.
"I knew I'd find you here," he said, his voice steady but softened by something close to concern.
Aleria, refusing to meet his eyes. "I just wanted to be left alone."

He picked his way down the rocks and crouched beside her, careful, like someone balancing two roles at once, the dutiful heir, and the brother who didn't want to frighten her off. His gaze flicked to the water and lingered there a moment. "It's fine if you come here. Just… don't tell people why. You know how they are." he murmured.
"I don't care what they say." She stared stubbornly at the glowing ripples. "It's warm because Yol is still down there. Because the stories are true." Ervius sighed, and sat down beside her, legs crossed to avoid the water. He took a deep breath. 
"That's exactly why they are sending you to the temple. You talk like this and the whole village thinks you're evil." 

Aleria's mouth opened, then closed again. She stared at the water, kicking at the ripples with her heel. For a heartbeat she looked as though she might argue, but the words caught in her throat. Her chest tightened, and her hands curled into fists in her lap. Tears welled in her eyes, and her voice broke as she blurted,
"I just wanted to know, that's all! Why is that so bad? I only asked! I just wanted to understand, but every time I do it's wrong. I don't get it, I don't get why it makes me evil." Ervius's expression faltered. The dutiful heir, the stern son their parents relied on, slipped for a moment, leaving only her brother. He reached out, hesitant at first, but then held his arm gently around her shoulders.
"You're not evil. Don't ever think that. You just… you notice things no one else does. And it scares them. Especially Mother. She doesn't know what to do with it, so she gets angry." He gave her shoulder a squeeze, awkward but sincere, his newly long arm pulling her in a little closer. 
"You're not wrong for asking 'why'. But the world doesn't forgive questions the way you think it should."

She finally turned to him, her black eyes glistening in the last of the light. Her voice was small, almost swallowed by the waves.  
"When I'm at the temple… will you come see me?" Ervius blinked, caught off guard, then managed a crooked smile. 
"Of course I will. I'll sneak away if I have to."
"You promise?" she pressed.
"I promise." He nudged her shoulder with his, still a little clumsy in his new height. "I'm not letting them keep me from my little sister, not even the priests." For a while they sat in silence, the ocean filling the hollow with its endless roar. Ervius kept his arm around her shoulders, until her sobs eased into quiet sniffles.
"Whatever you believe, Aleria," he said at last, gentler now, "be careful with your words. They'll punish you for them, and I'd rather not see that happen. Just… promise me you'll be careful and let me do the worrying."
