The shutters rattled against the salt wind, the old hinges complaining every time a stronger gust forced its way through. The room smelled of wet timber, ink, and the faint tang of brine that clung to everything in this part of the city. Maps of the Erellia and Memento Mori hung limp on the wall, their edges curled from the sea air. Lerran studied everyone at the long oak table, the surface of which had been scarred with years of knife marks and burn rings. Everyone's coats hung over chair-backs, dripping seawater on the floorboards. Even with the windows shut, you could hear the gulls outside screeching for the rain. There were only five in the room, but the tense emotions made the room feel even smaller, almost crowded. It was a bit unnerving having everyone's eyes on him, even after all these years he wasn't used to Commander Vass's stare, with one eye blind and both of the tieflings' horns broken off at the base. The same war-torn look could be seen on the rest of the people in the room. Major Iriess had plenty of scars to show from all the years in the city guard. Captain Harven, Lieutenant Marrek and himself had all been on the battlefield for the last battle of Memento Mori, they all had their share of horrors brought home from that war. 

None spoke until Lerran opened the folder. The papers inside were swollen and warped at the edges from years of handling. Ten years' worth of watching one man. Commander Serina leaned back in her chair, arms crossed, her eye fixed on the folder in front of him.  
"Let's get it over with, Lieutenant Lerran. From the top."  
"Subject: Captain Felen Corewood," he began, his voice carrying over the creak of the old house. "Status: Missing since last week. This is an overview, to make sure we're all on the same page before we decide next steps." He glanced around the table before turning to the first page.  

"1005 A.A. - *Medal Ceremony*. Location: Melbourne Holy Street. In recognition of his actions during the southern front's final engagement, Captain Corewood was awarded the Ribbon of Scarla. Ceremony notable for unusually high public turnout, given the losses suffered in the war. Public reception: overwhelmingly positive. Several speeches made reference to his 'unshakeable courage' and 'divine favour'. No incidents reported." Across the table, Commander Serina gave a short nod.  
"We needed that ceremony. Everyone did. Only thing that was a real victory and not a pity prize." Lerran acknowledged the comment with a brief nod, rain tapping steadily against the glass, and turned the page.

"1006 to 1007 - *Early Observations*. Corewood seen walking the harbour front on a number of evenings. Interactions with civilians were polite, though at times distracted. Dockworkers report he occasionally paused mid-conversation to then ask if there was someone else nearby, sometimes asking if they 'heard it', quote, 'the low echoing voice'. Witnesses assumed it was a turn of phrase or a joke - no indication of intoxication. Pattern consistent for approximately eighteen months." Major Iriess tapped the table with a knuckle. 
"He was still himself then. Quiet, maybe. Not dangerous."  
"No argument," Lerran said, already moving on before anyone else could speak.  

"1008 - *Tavern Incident*. Multiple civilian witnesses confirm Corewood entered The Blue Bell after midnight. Sat alone for roughly an hour, drinking little. Several patrons claim he spoke with a foreign sailor of unconfirmed origin, and that midway through the conversation his tone shifted, described as 'colder' or 'more distant'. No evidence of hostility, the sailor simply left early, looking unsettled. Corewood remained seated for hours before leaving without paying. Tavern-keeper reports the conversation centred on the stars and whether they 'looked different now'." Captain Harven leaned forward. 
"That was the first one that made it into Command's reports, wasn't it?"  
"It was," Lerran confirmed. "The tavern keep was very upset and demanded payment for the drinks, saying: 'even war heroes need to pay'." He tapped the folder. "From here on, things get… stranger, but still nothing actionable." 

"1009 - *Domestic Change*. Early spring: spouse departed the marital home" 
"That's when she took the girl," Harven muttered.  
"...Yes," Lerran confirmed without looking up, "taking their child to her sister's residence inland. She has since avoided all public appearances with him. Neighbours report the departure followed weeks of Corewood walking the streets at odd hours, returning near sunrise. Behaviour described as 'not like himself'. A few said he sometimes seemed distracted or confused about the time of day, but never aggressive." The room went quiet except for the wind pressing against the shutters. 
"Any confirmation on what she knows?" Vass asked.  
"None," Lerran said. "She's refused all interviews."  
"Most would assume he had a mistress on the side," Captain Harven said with a sneer. "Would've been easier to explain than the truth."  
"Whatever Corewood did after that day the public justified it with 'broken-hearted' or that 'he is having a rough time'." Lerran raised his voice slightly, drawing their attention back.  

"1008-1015 - *Unexplained Absences from Desk Duty*. Occasional reports from between these years note Corewood being absent from his assigned administrative post; when confronted, he gave vague or inconsistent explanations, usually citing personal errands or poor sleep." 

"1010, 1012 and 1014 -*Tavern Sightings.* Civilians report Corewood sitting alone for hours in different taverns, occasionally muttering to himself or staring into his drink. Witnesses generally assumed he was tired, bored, or simply avoiding company. People spread rumours that he had lost a bit of himself in the war and the rest when his wife left, and that was why he seemed withdrawn." Lerran flipped forward through several pages; the pattern remained: sightings at odd hours, generally unremarkable but unusual for someone of his standing, always leaving before anyone could press him.

"1011 A.A. - *Personality Shift Incident*. This was filed by Desk Officer Bree. Corewood, mid-conversation about supply rosters, reportedly 'looked right through me' and said, 'You wouldn't understand the weight of centuries', before continuing as though nothing had happened. Bree believed it was a strange turn of phrase, but still logged it. Corewood denies recollection." The officers exchanged uneasy glances.

"1006, 1012 and 1014 - *Religious Notes.* Many minor incidents with local temples. In 1006, banned from a Lirrus shrine after being seen arguing with one of the clerics on the street. In 1012, seen asking around for any holy symbols of Chrona. And in 1014, reports of him purchasing bone chalk, but this was later dismissed as unsubstantiated rumour."  

"1010, 1012 and 1014 - *Coincidental Civilian Contacts*. In three separate cases, Corewood was noted to have spoken with civilians who later died in unrelated incidents. The timing was coincidental and there was no suggestion of involvement, but it was noted in the file." Major Thorne spoke up. 
"And yet Command still wouldn't bring him in."  
"You know why," Harven snapped. "Every schoolchild still learns about the events leading up to that one battle and him getting that  ribbon. You drag him in without proof, you're telling half the country their war hero needs guarding." Lerran raised his voice again. 
"1015 - *CURRENT STATUS*." He waited until they had calmed and repeated:  
"1015 - *Current Status*. Corewood had been seen wandering at night more frequently and seeming more distracted than before, last seen near the southern shores at dusk, speaking with an unidentified figure. Witness describes the other as 'tall, wearing a dark coat, pale face'. The figure reportedly stepped into the shadows of the seawall and did not reappear. Corewood did not return home that night. He has not been seen since. We only found out when Corewood failed to report to his desk post for two days. Technically, as an active captain, he cannot simply vanish, regulations require we account for his whereabouts. Given his public standing, we also cannot be seen to ignore it." 

Lerran set the stack of papers down and looked around the table. "Ten years of keeping him on a leash. Ten years of buying silence, spinning tales of the 'lightning reaver', for what? To have him parade once a year as a memory of a fiasco. When all we’ve really done is make sure he doesn’t vanish into some back alley and make the papers for the wrong reasons. He’s still on the books, still wears the rank, and active captains can’t just disappear without someone asking questions."
"Calm yourself." Serina's voice cut across the room. "Harven, any ideas on how to find Corewood again?"  Harven leaned back. 
"Put the word out to the watch. Quietly. Make it an internal matter. If anyone in uniform, both the local guards and any military active or not, spot him, they report straight to us. And send out a small group to search the city. With his history, he will be nearby."  
"Agreed," Vass said. "And if the public asks?"  
Lerran closed the folder with a sigh. "We tell them their hero is on leave."  

The door slammed open so hard the hinges screamed. Rain and cold air burst in with the courier, streaked with soot, eyes wide.  
"A letter from Melbourne. The city was attacked." The courier took one long breath. "A fire giant appeared in the city, the whole city is gone, all burned down." 
Everyone at the table shot up from their seats, but no one said anything, all in shock. Serina broke the silence. "Disregard my order for the search party for Corewood. Send out the order still, if anyone sees him, report, but I doubt anyone will remember now when we have more important things to focus on. Gather all military, bring in anyone on leave, we need to help Melbourne."
